## The best team in the league came to Gillette

San Diego arrived on a four-game winning streak. They were the best team in the NWSL and still are. Patão called them "the best team in the league right now" after the match. You don't get credit for holding them scoreless for 62 minutes, but you should. We did.

Traoré gave them problems in the first half. We pressed. 10,213 showed up and it felt loud. For an hour it genuinely felt like we could get something out of this.

We couldn't finish, and then one moment of quality ended it.

## The goal

63rd minute. Ascanio drives through the middle and plays it through to Ludmila. One touch to go around Ansbrow. Curled into the far post. That's it.

You can't do much about a finish like that. A world-class player making one world-class decision. Her first goal for the Wave. Worth watching again just to appreciate it, even if it's the goal that beat us.

Worth noting: Ansbrow picked up a yellow in the 58th minute for a foul on Ludmila just outside the box. Five minutes later, Ludmila is one-on-one with her again. That timing stings.

## The foul problem is getting harder to ignore

Four games in and we're already leading the league in fouls. Against San Diego we gave away 19 to their 13. Two more yellow cards on the night. And Stevens wasn't even out there — she was suspended after the league upgraded her yellow from Utah to a red.

I genuinely don't know yet what kind of team this is going to be. We're four games in. But if physical and aggressive ends up being our identity, I can live with that. What I can't live with is giving away dumb fouls in dangerous areas, collecting cards that turn into suspensions, and handing opponents penalties. That's not hard-nosed defending — that's just shooting ourselves in the foot. Figure out where the line is and stay on the right side of it.

## Eight shots, zero on target

That's the one that stings. We were around their box all night. Freeman barely worked up a sweat. Traoré had her moments in the first half and none of them went in.

Close doesn't count.

## What to take from it

We went toe-to-toe with the best team in the league and were in it for an hour. The margin was one moment of brilliance. For a brand new club that's still figuring out how to compete at this level, that's real — even if it doesn't feel like it right now.
